\subsection{Prior set-up}\label{app:prior}
For the prior setup, we closely follow \cite{hauzenberger2020effectiveness}. Our priors are designed to provide sufficient regularization of the VAR coefficients to reduce estimation uncertainty. On the VAR coefficients, we therefore apply a Horseshoe (HS) prior \citep{carvalho2010horseshoe}, which belongs to the class of global-local shrinkage techniques. To outline the mechanism of this prior, it proves useful to collect the state-specific coefficients in an $M \times N$-dimensional matrix $\bm A_k = (\bm c_k, \bm A_{k1}, \dots, \bm A_{kP})$, for $k = \{0, 1\}$, so that $N = MP+1$. In the following, let $\bm a_k = \text{vec}(\bm A_k)$ be a $K (= NM)$-dimensional vector, then, for $k = \{0, 1\}$, then the hierarchical HS prior on the $j$th ($j = 1, \dots, K)$ state-specific coefficients is as follows:
\begin{equation*}
a_{kj} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau_k \lambda_{kj}), \quad \sqrt{\tau_k} \sim \mathcal{C}^+(0,1) \quad \text{and} \quad \sqrt{\lambda_{kj}} \sim \mathcal{C}^+(0,1).   
\end{equation*}
Here, a global component $\tau_k$ pushes all coefficients in $\bm a_k$ toward zero (i.e., the prior mean), while local scaling parameters $\lambda_{kj}$ are regime- and covariate-specific, providing sufficient flexibility.  Both hyperparameters $\sqrt{\tau_{k}}$ and $\sqrt{\lambda_{kj}}$ follow a half-Cauchy distribution denoted by $\mathcal{C}^+$. This prior is able to introduce sufficient sparsity via the global shrinkage component in a highly parameterized model that would otherwise be prone to overfitting. The local scales still allow for the detection of important individual covariates by mitigating the potential (over)shrinkage of the global parameter. 

The prior specification on the variance-covariance $\bm \Sigma$ is standard. Here, $\bm \Sigma$ follows an inverted Wishart distribution: 
\begin{equation*}
    \bm \Sigma \sim \mathcal{IW}(d_0, \bm D_0), 
\end{equation*}
with the scalar $d_0$ denoting the prior degrees of freedom and the $M\times M$-dimensional matrix $\bm D_0$ referring to the prior scaling. In the empirical application, we specify $d_0 = M+2$ and $\bm D_0 = 0.01 \bm I_M$. This choice ensures that the prior is proper, but still weakly informative.

To complete our proposed setup, we also need to specify the treatment of the hyperparameters $\mu$ and $\rho$, which define the transition between the two regimes.
\rev{Both are calibrated, and we document why. The threshold parameter is fixed at
\begin{equation*}
    \mu = 0,
\end{equation*}
expressed in units of the unstandardised stance $z_t$, i.e., the restrictive regime prevails whenever the federal funds rate exceeds the natural rate. This choice reflects an identification failure that we establish directly: the conditional likelihood is monotone in $\mu$ over the observed range of the stance and possesses no interior optimum. When $\mu$ is assigned a uniform prior over the full range of $z_t^*$, its posterior concentrates at the sample minimum, which implies a degenerate state allocation with almost the entire sample classified as restrictive; under a narrower uniform prior (e.g., $\bar z^* \pm 0.5$, our earlier specification), the posterior instead piles up at the lower bound of whatever support is chosen. Any bounded prior therefore effectively returns its own boundary, so that a nominally ``estimated'' $\mu$ would in truth be set by an arbitrary prior bound. Calibrating $\mu$ at the neutral-stance level replaces this hidden choice with a transparent and economically meaningful one.}
On the speed of adjustment parameter $\rho$ we impose a Gamma prior:
\begin{equation*}
    \rho \sim \mathcal{G}(s_0, s_1),
\end{equation*}
with the hyperparameters \rev{$s_0=4/0.001$ and $s_1=1/0.001$ (prior mean $4$, prior standard deviation $0.06$)} chosen such that the transition between the two states occurs relatively fast. This informative choice ensures a sensible state allocation\rev{, with the clearly expansionary and clearly restrictive episodes well separated while the transition periods between them are smooth} (see also \autoref{fig:stallo} for the estimated state allocation). \rev{Centering the prior at $7$ instead of $4$ leaves the state allocation and all impulse responses virtually unchanged; like $\mu$, the transition speed is deliberately calibrated, as the likelihood is uninformative about $\rho$.}

\subsection{Posterior quantities and sampling algorithm}\label{app:mcmc}
Next, we outline the main steps of our Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm, which is used to simulate from the full posterior distribution. To obtain the conditional posteriors, we combine the priors with the likelihood of the model, as described in \autoref{eq:STVAR} and \autoref{eq:St}. For most parameters, this leads to well-known conditional distributions, and simulating from them is straightforward. Let the symbol $\bullet$ indicate that we condition on the remaining parameters of the system, then these sampling steps read as follows: 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For simplicity we collect all VAR coefficients in a $2K$-dimensional vector $\bm a = (\bm a_1', \bm a_0')'$ and define the corresponding $2K$-dimensional vector of predictors $\bm x_t = \left((1, z_t, \bm y_{t-1}', \dots, \bm y_{t-p}')\times S_t,  (1, z_t, \bm y_{t-1}', \dots, \bm y_{t-p}') \times (1-S_t)\right)'$. 
    In what follows, we sample $\bm a$ from a multivariate Gaussian distribution: 
    \begin{equation*}
        \bm a|\bullet \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\bar{\bm a}, \bar{\bm V} \right),
    \end{equation*}
    with $\bar{\bm a}$ denoting the posterior mean and $\bar{\bm V}$ the posterior variance-covariance matrix: 
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
       \bar{\bm V} =& \left(\bm \Sigma^{-1} \otimes \bm X'\bm X + \underline{\bm V}^{-1} \right), \\
       \bar{\bm a} =&  \bar{\bm V}^{-1}  \left(\bm \Sigma^{-1} \otimes \bm X'\text{vec}(\bm Y) \right). \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}
    Here, $\bm X = (\bm x_1, \dots, \bm x_T)'$ and $\bm Y = (\bm y_1, \dots, \bm y_T)'$ denote the full data matrices and $\underline{\bm V} = \text{diag}\left(\{v_{1j}\}_{j=1}^{K}, \{v_{0j}\}_{j=1}^{K}\right)$, where $v_{kj} = \tau_k \lambda_{kj}$, collects the prior variances of all coefficients on the main diagonal. 
    \item To draw the HS scaling parameters we rely on the following steps, outlined in \cite{makalic2015simple}: 
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tau_k|\bullet &\sim \mathcal{IG}\left(\frac{K + 1}{2}, \frac{1}{\phi_k} + \sum_{j = 1}^{K} \frac{a_{kj}}{2\lambda_{kj}}\right), \\
        \lambda_{kj}|\bullet &\sim \mathcal{IG}\left(1, \frac{1}{\zeta_{kj}}+ \frac{a_{kj}}{2 \tau_k}\right), \\
    \end{aligned} 
    \end{equation*}
    where the two auxiliary variables $\phi_k$ and $\zeta_{kj}$ are also drawn from inverted Gamma distributions: 
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \phi_k|\bullet &\sim \mathcal{IG}\left(1,1+\frac{1}{\tau_k} \right), \\
        \zeta_{kj}|\bullet &\sim \mathcal{IG}\left(1,1+\frac{1}{\lambda_{kj}} \right).
    \end{aligned} 
    \end{equation*}
    \item We sample $\bm \Sigma$ from an inverted Wishart distribution: 
    \begin{equation*}
        \bm \Sigma|\bullet \sim \mathcal{IW}(d_1, \bm D_1),
    \end{equation*} 
    with posterior degrees of freedom $d_1 = d_0 + T/2$ and $M \times M$-dimensional posterior scaling matrix $\bm D_1 = \bm D_0 + (\bm Y - \bm X \bm a)'(\bm Y - \bm X \bm a)/2 $.
    \item \rev{With $\mu$ calibrated (see \autoref{app:prior}), the speed of adjustment $\rho$ is the only transition parameter left to sample. It is drawn with a random-walk Metropolis-within-Gibbs step:} the candidate value is sampled from a Gaussian proposal density $\rho^{(*)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\rho^{(s)},  \kappa_{\rho})$, centered on the last accepted draw $\rho^{(s)}$ with variance $\kappa_{\rho}$. The acceptance probability of the proposed value is then given by:
    \begin{equation*}
        \min \left(1, \frac{\mathcal{L}(\rho^{(*)}|\bullet)\, p(\rho^{(*)})}{\mathcal{L}(\rho^{(s)}|\bullet)\, p(\rho^{(s)})} \right),
    \end{equation*}
    with $\mathcal{L}$ denoting the data likelihood conditional on the proposed value $\rho^{(*)}$ and the last accepted draw $\rho^{(s)}$, while $p(\cdot)$ refers to the prior evaluated at the respective value. \rev{The proposal variance $\kappa_\rho$ is tuned adaptively during the burn-in period toward an acceptance rate of $0.3$, based on the acceptance frequency over rolling windows of $50$ iterations, and is held fixed thereafter so that the retained draws stem from a time-homogeneous chain. In the baseline specification the realised acceptance rate is $0.33$.}
    \rev{In practice, we evaluate $\mathcal{L}$ exactly (up to a proportionality constant that does not depend on $\rho$) by whitening the system with the error precision matrix. Let $\bm L$ denote the lower Cholesky factor of $\bm\Sigma^{-1} = \bm L \bm L'$ and let $\bm U(\rho) = \bm Y - \bm X(\rho)\, \bm A$ collect the reduced-form residuals implied by the candidate transition parameter. The rows of the transformed residual matrix $\bm U(\rho)\, \bm L$ are standard Gaussian under the model, so that $\log \mathcal{L}(\rho \mid \bullet) = -\frac{1}{2}\lVert \bm U(\rho)\, \bm L \rVert_F^2 + \text{const}$, where $\lVert \cdot \rVert_F$ is the Frobenius norm. This makes the Metropolis acceptance ratio an exact evaluation of the conditional posterior of $\rho$.}\footnote{\rev{Note for revision (not for publication): the final code is \texttt{Codes/STVARestimation.R} in the replication package (July 2026). Relative to the originally submitted implementation it corrects three issues. (i) Whitening: the earlier code whitened with the transposed factor $\bm L'$ instead of $\bm L$, leaving the transformed residual covariance different from the identity and the acceptance ratio inexact; impulse responses and the state allocation are virtually unchanged by the correction (posterior-median IRF correlations $\geq 0.997$), but under the exact likelihood the posterior of $\rho$ essentially coincides with its informative prior, i.e., the transition speed is calibrated, not data-identified. (ii) Proposal tuning: $\mu$ and $\rho$ previously shared a single joint proposal with a common adaptive scaling; the $\mu$-component of the proposal ended up far too small, so the $\mu$-chain moved negligibly from its starting value and its apparent posterior merely echoed the initialisation, while the acceptance rate ($\approx 0.6$) looked deceptively healthy. Sampling the parameters in separate blocks with individually tuned scales revealed the monotone-likelihood problem documented in \autoref{app:prior}, which motivates calibrating $\mu$. (iii) Draw retention: a post-processing step intended to retain the most stable draws was inoperative (it sorted on a criterion that is constant by construction, the companion-form root including the intercept) and effectively kept the earliest post-burn-in draws; the final code thins evenly over the full chain. The calibration $\mu = 0$ reproduces the originally reported state allocation almost exactly (regime-path correlation $0.98$).}}
\end{enumerate}
\rev{We repeat these steps $12,000$ times and discard the initial $2,000$ draws as burn-in; the retained draws are thinned evenly to $2,000$ for inference. [Note for revision: iteration count and the treatment of $\rho$ and $\mu$ above have been harmonized with the replication code (\texttt{Codes/STVARestimation.R}; previously the text stated $15,000/5,000$ iterations, a $\rho$-prior mean of $7$, and a uniform prior for $\mu$, which is now calibrated at the neutral stance as documented in \autoref{app:prior}). The impulse responses are insensitive to these choices: across all combinations examined, posterior-median IRF correlations are $\geq 0.97$ with maximum deviations below $1.2$ basis points.]}
