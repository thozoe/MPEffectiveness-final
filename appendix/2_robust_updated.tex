\subsection{Lag length and real activity controls}
Both exercises in this subsection address the persistence \rev{of the estimated responses (see also the level responses in \autoref{fig:IRFcum})}. \autoref{fig:IRFlag8} reports the yield responses when the lag length is doubled from $p=4$ to $p=8$ weeks, and \autoref{fig:IRFwei} shows the responses from a specification that adds the (weekly) German activity index to $\bm y_t$, thereby allowing for feedback between financial markets and real activity at the weekly frequency. In both cases, the responses --- and in particular the differences across US policy regimes --- are very close to the baseline results, indicating that neither the lag order nor the absence of a real activity control drives the shape or the speed of mean reversion of the estimated responses.
