\noindent Dear Editors,

Please find attached our manuscript \textit{Hawks vs.\ Doves: The Fed's Stance and the Term Structure of ECB Policy Transmission}, co-authored with Niko Hauzenberger (University of Strathclyde) and Florian Huber (University of Salzburg), which we submit for your consideration for publication in the \textit{Journal of International Money and Finance}.

The paper asks whether the transmission of ECB monetary policy to euro area financial markets depends on the policy stance of the Federal Reserve. This question cannot be answered with a linear model, as the propagation of a domestic shock is invariant to the foreign policy environment by construction. Thus, we estimate a Bayesian smooth transition VAR on weekly euro area data for 2005-2025, in which the dynamic coefficients vary continuously with the deviation of the federal funds rate from the US neutral rate, and trace the response of the entire term structure to high-frequency identified ECB surprises across the full range of the Fed's stance.

Our central result is that the Fed's stance does not scale ECB transmission up or down: it \textit{rotates} it. Under an accommodative Fed, a contractionary ECB surprise raises euro area yields by a similar amount along the whole curve; under a restrictive Fed, the short end responds somewhat more strongly while the long end ceases to respond at all, so that state dependence in ECB transmission is a phenomenon of the long end. At the short end the composition changes instead: inflation compensation falls about three times more when the Fed is restrictive, so that real short-term financing conditions tighten by considerably more than nominal yields suggest. The sign and the maturity profile are robust to how the stance is measured and how the surprise is identified.

The paper sits at the intersection of international monetary policy spillovers, high-frequency identification and the term structure, and answers  a question of the post-pandemic Fed-ECB divergence: whether a central bank's  own transmission depends on what its largest counterpart is doing. We therefore believe it is well suited to the scope of the journal.

The manuscript supersedes an earlier draft circulated under the title \textit{Hawks vs.\ Doves: ECB's Monetary Policy in Light of the Fed's Policy Stance}, which now features an extended sample and has been substantially revised. It has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission. 

Thank you for your consideration. We look forward to hearing from you.
